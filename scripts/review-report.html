Practice your own mistakes

Blunder check trains the habit behind most of your lost games: before you let go of a piece, check what your opponent can take. Each position is a move you really played; about two in five lost material and the rest were safe even though something was attacked. Say which, then show how they win it. Twenty a round; aim for 18 right, then aim to answer faster.

Every position here comes from one of your games. Find the win: you had something much better than what you played. Spot the threat: your move left something open; find how your opponent punishes it. Openings: a position you reached while still in opening theory; play what strong players play. Click a piece, then the square it should go to.

Puzzles come back on a schedule. Right first time, a puzzle returns after 1, 3, 7, 16 and then 35 days; missed, it comes back later in the session and again tomorrow. Due is today's set: the reviews that are due plus up to 15 new puzzles, your #1 leak first. Your progress is kept in this browser.

Where the points go

Your mistakes and blunders, sorted by what actually happened on the board: the piece left hanging, the fork you walked into, the pin you missed. Inaccuracies are left out; they rarely decide games at this level.
